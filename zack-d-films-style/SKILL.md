## The formula — every video, no exceptions

1. **One question.** The title is the curiosity itself plus ONE emoji at the end: "How A Hidden Bookshelf Door Works 🤔", "Why Ducks Sleep With One Eye Open 🤨", "Turning Your Finger Into A Gun 😨". If the topic needs context to sound interesting, it is not a topic.
2. **No intro, no title card.** The first frame is a scene already in motion — a character mid-problem. Nothing branded, nothing announced.
3. **The explanation IS a mini-story.** A character needs something → uses or discovers the thing → the mechanism is revealed step by step → the payoff reveal lands on the final frame. Facts ride on the story, never the other way round.
4. **Length 27–40 seconds.** Narration ~60–80 words as ONE continuous flow: sentences run across shots, no pauses, no greeting, no "part 2", no call to action.
5. **Ending = the reveal.** The last frame holds the payoff (the hidden room, the result, the twist). No outro. The stillness of the final frame against the motion before it is what makes people loop and comment.

## Visual grammar (proven in the frame-by-frame file)

- **Locked recurring characters.** His current run uses the same girl (double buns, pink tank top) plus a tiny sidekick creature with giant ears across videos. Lock YOUR character sheet the same way and reuse it — recognition is the brand.
- **The camera ladder, always moving:** wide establishing → medium → close-up of the hands/action → MACRO of the mechanism → cutaway/X-ray of the internals → flat schematic/diagram view → back to wide for the payoff. Never two similar shots in a row; a cut or camera move every 1–2 seconds.
- **Cutaways are the signature.** The invisible is made visible: circuit boards, relay coils, magnetic locks, inside the body. If the narration names a hidden part, the picture must show that part at that second.
- **Highlight graphics.** Neon-green arrows, outlines and diamond markers appear on the exact part being explained; animated light pulses travel along wires and paths to show signals/energy moving.
- **In-scene branding.** His name is printed on the circuit-board chip inside the scene (18s of the reference video). For Zeesh this is the WhoaWire tag rule: the brand on a natural object inside the scene, placement varying per video — never an overlay logo.
- **Palette.** Bright, saturated environments (cyan/teal walls, warm wood, colorful props). The payoff space flips to dark + neon for contrast.
- **Real-feeling props.** Fake but readable in-world details (book covers with proper titles and cover art) sell the world. Never leave a prop blank or gibberish when the camera gets close.
- **Transitions.** Whip/motion-blur cuts into mechanism beats. No fades; black only as a deliberate inside-the-mechanism beat.

## Captions

- Bold white text with a black outline, bottom-center of the 9:16 frame.
- 2–4 word chunks, phrase by phrase, exactly synced to the narration — the caption stream is the narration itself, broken mid-sentence across shots ("As you pull / the book forward / it tilts / on a tiny hinge…").
- No emojis in captions, no punctuation piles, no on-screen title text. The title lives only in the YouTube title field.

## Narration and sound

- Calm, dramatic male narrator, even pace (~2–2.5 words per second), zero filler. Every sentence adds a new fact or the next step of the mechanism.
- Script shape: situation hook (often visual before the first word) → a cause-and-effect chain ("as you pull X, it tilts on Y, triggering Z…") where each step causes the next → one reveal line at the end.
- Sound bed is never empty: soft background music under everything, small emphasis sounds on mechanism beats.

## Topic selection

- Ask: "Would a stranger stop scrolling for this question?" Proven veins: your body ("What happens if…", how a body process works), everyday mechanisms (locks, doors, toilets, stitches, anchors), animals behaving strangely, weird-but-true history with a visual twist, "why" questions about ordinary things.
- One video = one question = one mechanism. Never two facts in one short.
- Plan topics in batches like he does (his research pages run months ahead): keep a rolling bank of 20+ approved questions so production never waits for an idea.

## Script skeleton (fill in, ~60–80 words total)

```
[0–5s, VISUAL HOOK — no words needed yet, or one short line]
"[Character] needed to [goal/problem]…"
[MECHANISM CHAIN — one clause per shot, each causing the next]
"As [action 1], it [effect 1] on a [hidden part 1],
triggering [hidden part 2]. And as [action 2] are moved into position,
their hidden [parts] change too, sending [signals/force/water] to [core part].
Once [condition completes], [final mechanism step] —
and [the door/body/machine] [does the payoff action]…"
[REVEAL LINE — one short sentence, lands on the last frame]
"…to reveal [the hidden thing]."
```

## Shot-list template (8–12 shots, follow the ladder)

| # | Size | Content | Narration clause |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Close/medium, in motion | Character mid-problem (the hook) | — or hook line |
| 2 | Wide | The place/object established | situation |
| 3 | Close-up | Hands start the action | "As you…" |
| 4 | Macro | First hidden part, highlighted | "…it tilts on a…" |
| 5 | Cutaway | Inside the mechanism | "…triggering…" |
| 6 | Schematic/diagram | The system view, glowing paths | "…sending signals to…" |
| 7 | Medium | Character completes the pattern | "Once the…" |
| 8 | Cutaway/macro | The final mechanism step | "…cuts power to…" |
| 9 | Wide | The payoff action happens | "…pushes it out…" |
| 10 | Final frame | The reveal, held | reveal line |

## Route A — Claude + Blender (his real pipeline)

His team works in Blender + Cascadeur + After Effects with templates and asset libraries. Zeesh's version: **Claude writes the plan and the Blender Python (bpy) automation; Blender does the 3D.** One-time setup, then every video is an assembly job.

**One-time setup (Claude prepares, Zeesh installs free software):**
1. Blender (free) + BlenderKit addon (free asset library) + Cascadeur free tier (physics-assisted character animation) — or keyframe by hand for simple moves.
2. A base `.blend` template Claude builds once: vertical 1080x1920 render settings (Eevee for speed), a simple room set, studio lighting rig, the locked character rig (a stock-style 3D human matching Zeesh's character sheet), camera presets named WIDE / MEDIUM / CLOSE / MACRO.
3. A materials/props shelf: wood, wall colors (his cyan/teal), bookcase, books with readable cover textures Claude generates as images.

**Per video (Claude does the thinking, Blender does the lifting):**
1. Claude writes the script (skeleton above) + the shot list.
2. Claude writes a `build.py` bpy script that: opens the template, builds/adjusts the set for this topic, places the mechanism parts (hinge, switch, board, spring…), positions the character, creates one camera per shot from the presets, and sets the timeline markers per shot.
3. Animate the few things that move: character actions in Cascadeur or simple keyframes; mechanism parts with keyframed rotations/positions; highlight graphics as emissive materials + arrow meshes Claude's script adds.
4. Cutaways: build the mechanism as a separate open-faced model (no front panel) — the "X-ray" is just a second version of the prop with the cover removed; schematic shots = top-down camera + emissive line curves for the glowing paths.
5. Render per shot (Eevee, 1080x1920), then assemble: cuts on the narration, captions 2–4 words bottom-center, music bed. Assembly can be Claude-driven ffmpeg or any free editor.
6. Narration: `/opt/hatch/bin/tts` (Casper) recorded FIRST — shot lengths are cut to the voice, never the other way round.

**Blender rules that keep it looking like his:** saturated flat colors, soft shadows, one bright key light; mechanism parts slightly oversized so they read at phone size; camera never static for more than 2 seconds; render a motion-blur pass on whip transitions.

## Route B — No Blender (AI generation, Zeesh's current pipeline)

1. Script first (skeleton above), narration recorded first (TTS Casper) so shot lengths are known.
2. Character sheet locked (front/side/expressions) — every generation references it; clothes, hair and sidekick identical in all shots.
3. Generate one image per shot (the exact frame the ladder demands), then image-to-video per shot with a motion prompt ("slow push-in", "hand pulls the book toward camera"…). Cutaway/schematic shots are generated as their own images (open mechanism, top-down diagram) — do not expect one clip to morph into a cutaway.
4. Highlight graphics (arrows, outlines, pulses) are added in the edit, not generated — draw them as overlays so they stay clean and readable.
5. Assemble vertical 9:16, master 1080x1920; captions chunked 2–4 words; music bed under everything; end on the reveal frame.
6. Verify the finished video with the `generated_video_verification` skill before delivering it.

**Route B failure modes to check:** character drift between shots (regenerate with the sheet, don't accept a near-match), gibberish text on props in close-ups (crop tighter or regenerate), morphing mechanisms (split the beat into two shots).

## QA checklist — fail the video if any answer is no

- Is the first frame already a scene in motion (no intro/title card)?
- Does every shot differ from the previous one in size or angle?
- Is the hidden mechanism SHOWN in a cutaway, not just described?
- Do highlight graphics point at the exact part the narration names?
- Are captions 2–4 word chunks, synced, bottom-center?
- Does it end on the reveal with no CTA or outro?
- Is it one question only, 27–40 seconds?
- Is the character identical in every shot (clothes, hair, sidekick)?
